Why credits beat one-dollar checkouts

Stripe's published US card rate is about 2.9% plus USD 0.30. A USD 1 charge loses roughly one third to payment fees. Sell USD 5 or USD 10 credit packs and deduct each workload from the balance.

Why Minecraft traffic must be measured

Cloudflare lists 5 GB of monthly Minecraft traffic and USD 1 per additional GB. Spectrum can cost more than a short session, so the free tier cannot receive unlimited protected traffic.

What changes for B2B and Argentina

WorkOS SSO starts around USD 125 per connection each month and belongs in a paid enterprise contract. Stripe's global availability list does not currently include a standard Argentina Payments account, so the operating company needs an eligible setup or another processor.

The market validates the pieces, not the full product.

Competitors already let agents deploy code, create databases, or run sandboxes. The opening is a server-enforced contract that combines price, approval, TTL, checkout, and operation across different workload types.

Most crowded starting point. Add it after the policy and checkout system proves useful.

No exact public match found.

No reviewed competitor combines Host, Deploy, and Continue with a hash-bound plan, server-side spending cap, checkout link, mandatory TTL, and cross-agent identity. That gap is useful, but it is not a moat by itself. Northflank can copy the breadth fastest. Railway can copy the agent experience fastest.

The roadmap stays narrow until people pay.

The vision spans Host, Deploy, and Continue. The first build does not. It proves one paid workspace flow and one public game-server flow before adding another deployment platform.

  1. Current

    Concept and system design

    • Product contract, workload model, Oracle topology, economics, and launch order are documented.
    • The Multica sandbox fork is technical reference material. It is not the product architecture.
    • No production control plane, scheduler, billing service, MCP server, or public template exists yet.
  2. Build 1

    Safe control plane

    • Ship catalog, plan, approve, apply, status, logs, extend, snapshot, restore, and destroy.
    • Bind approval to the exact plan hash. Enforce idempotency, spending caps, TTL, and scoped credentials on the server.
    • Expose the same contract through API, MCP, and CLI. Start with workspace.pi and game.minecraft-java.
  3. Build 2

    Closed beta on seven VPS

    • Place paid work before free work across six workers. Keep one VPS for Postgres, scheduling, events, and identity.
    • Add reverse runner connections, durable JSONL sessions, fencing generations, event replay, SSD quotas, snapshots, suspend, and resume.
    • Route Minecraft Java through a measured L4 path. Record traffic, RAM, I/O, queue time, and manual interventions.
  4. Build 3

    Identity, credits, and teams

    • Use AuthKit and auth.md to claim users and link agents. Add organization policies only when a team needs them.
    • Sell prepaid credits through an eligible payment provider. Return checkout, balance, usage, and receipts to the conversation.
    • Add budgets, roles, audit events, and team-level usage without building a dashboard.
  5. After proof

    Deploy and expand capacity

    • Add git-web and Postgres only after Continue retains users and the approval contract works under load.
    • Add SSO, SCIM, arbitrary images, Firecracker, high availability, and more regions only when paid demand covers them.
    • Do not promise production SLA, native cross-agent session migration, or unlimited free game traffic before those systems exist.

Two real flows are enough.

The pilot ships Continue with workspace.pi and Host with game.minecraft-java. Deploy remains a demonstrated flow in the landing page, not a production promise, until the control plane and billing loop survive real usage.
